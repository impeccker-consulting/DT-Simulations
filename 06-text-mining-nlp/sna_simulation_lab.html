01 · Foundations

What is a network?

A social network is just two things: actors (people, brands, teams — drawn as dots, called nodes) and relationships between them (drawn as lines, called edges or ties). That's it. Everything in this lab — influence, gatekeeping, pricing power — is derived from nothing more than where the dots are and which lines connect them.

Click any two dots to connect or disconnect them

Ties here are undirected — if A is connected to B, the tie runs both ways. Real platforms are messier: on Instagram, you can follow someone who doesn't follow you back. That distinction — directed vs undirected ties — matters later, when we get to influencer pricing.

02 · A whole-network metric

Density: how tightly knit is the group?

Density asks: of every relationship that could exist in this group, how many actually do? A team where everyone talks to everyone is dense. A team where people only talk to their direct manager is sparse.

05 · Group-level metrics

The shape of the whole network

Individual scores are useful, but a manager also needs to describe the whole network's shape. Two organisations can have identical headcount and average connections, yet behave completely differently depending on their structure.

Why this matters to a manager: a hub-and-spoke structure is fast to coordinate but has a single point of failure — remove the hub and the network fragments. A decentralised mesh is slower to align but survives the loss of any one person. This is the exact same trade-off you're making when you decide whether one influencer should own a campaign, or you should spread budget across several well-connected but ordinary-looking accounts.
06 · Applied SNA

What should you pay an Instagram influencer?

The naive answer is "pay per follower." But followers are just degree centrality — raw reach, nothing else. A brand that only pays for reach is ignoring three things SNA measures directly: how engaged that reach actually is, how influential the influencer's own followers are (eigenvector centrality), and whether the influencer bridges audiences a brand couldn't otherwise reach (betweenness centrality). All figures below are in Indian Rupees (₹) and are illustrative teaching numbers, not market benchmarks.

price = base rate × reach × (engagement multiplier) × (amplification multiplier) × (bridging multiplier)
reachfollowers ÷ 1,000, priced at a base rate per 1,000 people reached
engagementcomments + likes as a % of followers → is anyone actually paying attention?
amplificationeigenvector centrality → are their followers themselves well-connected / influential?
bridgingbetweenness centrality → do they connect audiences/communities a brand can't otherwise reach?

That reversal is the whole pitch for using SNA in influencer marketing: reach is visible, network position is not — and network position is where the real value hides.
